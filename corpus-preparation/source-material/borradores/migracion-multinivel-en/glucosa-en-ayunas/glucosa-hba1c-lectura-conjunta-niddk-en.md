# Fasting glucose and HbA1c: how to read them together

<!-- vitamap:block summary -->
## In one sentence

Fasting glucose and HbA1c view glucose regulation through different time
windows: one measures a particular moment, while the other approximates
average exposure over the preceding months.

<!-- vitamap:block analogy -->
## A picture to begin with

Imagine two ways of observing a journey. Fasting glucose resembles a photograph
taken early in the morning: it shows one instant under defined conditions.
HbA1c resembles a long exposure: it summarizes a trace accumulated over about
three months.

The image distinguishes their time scales. It does not mean that HbA1c
reconstructs every day or that fasting glucose describes everything before and
after the blood draw.

<!-- vitamap:block literal -->
## What it actually means

Fasting plasma glucose measures glucose concentration in a sample collected
after at least eight hours without food or drinks other than water. It is a
point measurement and is often taken in the morning.

HbA1c reports the proportion of hemoglobin with glucose attached and is used
as an approximation of average glucose over roughly the previous three months.
It does not require fasting. Conditions affecting red blood cells or
hemoglobin can alter its reliability.

<!-- vitamap:block relations -->
## How they relate

Both tests can help detect glucose disorders and contribute to diagnosis, but
they are not interchangeable. Reading them together contrasts one defined
moment with an approximate longer-term average.

NIDDK explains that the tests do not always identify the same people. Fasting
glucose may fall within one general range while HbA1c falls within another, or
vice versa. This discrepancy does not by itself prove that a test is wrong; it
usually leads to a review of conditions, possible interferences, and the need
for repeat or confirmatory testing.

<!-- vitamap:block limitations -->
## Limits of this explanation

The “snapshot and long exposure” comparison simplifies two different
measurements. HbA1c is not an exact film of every day, and fasting glucose does
not directly measure how the pancreas or liver functions.

Together, the tests still do not diagnose diabetes or prediabetes
automatically, distinguish the type of diabetes, or establish a cause. A
general range cannot be applied to one person without the report and clinical
context.

<!-- vitamap:block deep_dive -->
## If you want to go deeper

Fasting glucose has biological variation and depends on fasting conditions,
the timing of collection, and sample processing. HbA1c integrates a longer
window but may be less reliable in pregnancy, some anemias, or with certain
hemoglobin variants.

Because of these differences, discordant results may provide real information
rather than cancel each other out. NIDDK explains that when different tests do
not agree in a diagnostic context, the test above the diagnostic threshold is
confirmed and the whole picture is assessed. This general rule does not
replace evaluation of an individual case.
